Webflow or custom code: which fits your company

For a marketing website, the right choice depends less on technology than on who will run the site after launch. A plain comparison of cost, speed, editing and SEO.

We build websites in Webflow, and we also write custom code when a project needs it, so this is not a sales pitch for one side. It is the comparison we walk clients through when they ask which to choose.

The short answer: for a marketing website that your team needs to update, Webflow is usually the better fit. For a product, a web app, or a site with heavy custom logic, custom code usually is. Most of the decision comes down to four questions.

Who will edit the site after launch?

This is the question that decides most projects. A marketing website changes constantly: new pages, new posts, a price, a photo, a team member. If every change needs a developer, changes get slower and rarer, and the site goes stale.

Webflow gives non-developers a visual editor and a CMS for content that repeats, such as posts, case studies, services or team members. Your team edits the real page, not a form that hopes to look like it. With custom code, editing is whatever the developers build, often a separate content management system that someone has to set up, host and maintain.

What does it cost over time?

Compare the total over a few years, not the launch invoice.

  • Webflow has a recurring plan fee for hosting and the CMS, and the build cost. Hosting, security updates and the editor are included, and most content changes need no developer.
  • Custom code has hosting costs that can be low, but someone has to maintain the servers, dependencies and security updates, and most changes need a developer. Those hours add up.

Check current Webflow plan prices on Webflow's own site before you decide, because they change. Then estimate how often you change the site and what a developer's hour costs you. For many companies, that maintenance line decides it.

How fast can it launch?

Webflow removes the work of setting up hosting, deployment and a content system, so the time goes into design and content. Most of the marketing sites we build go live 6 to 10 weeks from kickoff. A custom-coded site of the same scope usually needs more engineering time for the same result, unless it reuses a system the team already has.

Is one better for SEO?

Neither platform ranks a page on its own. Search engines and AI assistants reward pages that answer real questions clearly, load quickly, and are built with clean, accessible markup and structured data. Both approaches can deliver that.

The practical difference is control. In Webflow, page titles, descriptions, URLs, redirects, sitemaps and structured data are settings your team can change. With custom code, each of those is a feature someone builds, and a change waits for a developer. What matters most is that someone keeps doing the work after launch.

When custom code is the better choice

  • The site is really an application: user accounts, dashboards, complex search or calculations.
  • It needs deep, two-way integration with internal systems that a CMS cannot hold.
  • You have strict requirements on where data is stored or how the infrastructure is run.
  • You already have an engineering team that will own and maintain it.

The middle path

It does not have to be one or the other. A common setup is a Webflow marketing site, edited by the marketing team, with custom code where it earns its place: an embedded tool, an integration, or a separate web app on its own subdomain. You get easy editing where content changes often, and engineering where it is genuinely needed.

A quick way to decide

If most of what changes on your site is content, and the people changing it are not developers, choose Webflow. If most of what changes is functionality, choose custom code. If it is both, split it.
